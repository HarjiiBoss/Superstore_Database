Analysis

What the Data Reveals

Revenue Risk
50.8% of Order Lines Lose Money — The Biggest Operational Risk
More than half of all 8,399 order lines in this dataset are loss-making. Furniture has the highest loss rate at 53.5% — more than 1 in 2 Furniture order lines lose money. Loss-making lines are the norm, not an edge case.
01
Margin Alert
Furniture Earns 2.3% Margin on $5.18M in Sales
Furniture is the second-highest revenue category but delivers only 2.3% profit margin — the weakest of all three categories. Technology earns 14.8% on $5.98M and Office Supplies earns 13.8% on $3.75M. Furniture's revenue disguises a near-zero return on every dollar sold.
02
Discount Impact
Too Few Discounted Orders to Set a Discount Threshold
Order lines with no discount average $249 profit. The two order lines in the 21–30% band average –$249, but only 5 order lines carry a discount above 10%. Almost all order lines sit at 0–10% discount, so the data cannot show where discounting turns unprofitable.
03
Revenue Trend
2009's $4.21M Revenue Peak Has Not Been Recovered
Revenue fell 15.7% in 2010 and a further 3.2% in 2011, to $3.44M, before recovering +8.2% in 2012. The business generated $3.72M in 2012 — still $489K below the 2009 peak.
04
Seasonal Pattern
December and January Are Peak Months — June Is the Low Point
December accounts for 9.84% of sales and January 9.69% — the two strongest months. June is the weakest at 6.91%, and May to August all sit below the monthly average of 8.3%.
05
Regional Performance
West Leads Revenue but Not Margin
The West region generates the most revenue at $3.6M but delivers only an 8.26% margin — below the regional average. Northwest Territories has the highest margin at 12.57%, followed by Atlantic at 11.86% and Ontario at 11.32% on $3.06M. Nunavut is the weakest market at 2.44% margin on $116K.
06
Critical Finding

The 50.8% loss rate on order lines is the single most important number in this dataset: profit comes from roughly half the order lines while the other half lose money. Furniture pricing, with a 2.3% margin and a 53.5% loss rate, is the clearest place to start a margin review.

Business Questions

10 Self-Generated Questions & Answers

BQ-01
How has total revenue trended year-over-year?
Revenue peaked at $4.21M in 2009 and declined for two consecutive years, to $3.44M in 2011 (−18.3%). A partial recovery of +8.2% in 2012 still left the business $489K below its 2009 peak.
BQ-02
Which product category generates the highest profit margin?
Technology leads at 14.8% ($886K profit). Office Supplies earns 13.8%. Furniture earns only 2.3% despite generating $5.18M in revenue.
BQ-03
Which sub-categories are destroying profit (bottom 5)?
Tables lost –$99.1K on $1.90M in sales (–5.2% margin). Four sub-categories made a net loss: Tables and Bookcases in Furniture, and Scissors, Rulers & Trimmers and Rubber Bands in Office Supplies.
BQ-04
Does a higher discount always lead to lower profit?
The data cannot say. Order lines with 0% discount average $249 profit and the two order lines at 21–30% average –$249, but only 5 order lines carry a discount above 10%.
BQ-05
Which shipping mode is most cost-efficient?
Regular Air carries 74.7% of order lines at 0.64% shipping cost as a percentage of sales. Delivery Truck has the highest cost per order line ($45.35) and the highest share of sales (0.83%), though it carries the largest order lines.
BQ-06
Which customer segment is most profitable?
Corporate generates the most total profit at $600K. Small Business leads on margin at 11.32%. Consumer has the highest average order value at $2,847.
BQ-07
Which regions underperform on margin relative to sales?
West leads in revenue ($3.6M) but earns only 8.26% margin — below average. Northwest Territories has the highest margin at 12.57%. Nunavut is the worst market at 2.44% margin on $116K in sales.
BQ-08
What share of order lines result in a net loss, by category?
50.8% of all 8,399 order lines lose money (4,264 lines). Furniture has the highest loss rate at 53.5% and the largest total loss at –$436K — the single biggest operational risk in the dataset.
BQ-09
Is there a seasonal sales pattern by month?
December (9.84% of sales) and January (9.69%) are the two peak months. June is the weakest at 6.91%.
BQ-10
Do returned orders follow a pattern by category or region?
Technology in Ontario has the highest return rate at 13.73%, followed by Furniture in Yukon at 13.64%. Furniture's combination of high returns, 2.3% margin, and 53.5% loss rate makes it the highest-risk category overall.
Business Insights

Structured Analytical Insights

01
Margin Strategy
Furniture Is a Revenue Trap — High Sales, Near-Zero Return
Furniture generates $5.18M in revenue — the second-highest category — but converts this into only $117K in profit at a 2.3% margin. Combined with a 53.5% loss rate and a high return rate, the category is absorbing disproportionate operational cost without delivering commensurate return. A full pricing and cost review of Furniture sub-categories — particularly Tables, which lost $99K — is the single highest-leverage intervention available.
02
Discount Policy
Test Discount Levels Before Setting a Policy
Order lines with no discount average $249 profit and the two order lines at 21–30% average –$249, but 5 order lines above 10% are too few to set a cap. Before setting a discount policy, the business needs more order lines at higher discount levels, ideally from a planned test.
03
Portfolio Prioritisation
Double Down on Technology — It Earns 14.8% Margin at Scale
Technology generates $886K in profit on $5.98M in sales at a 14.8% margin — the highest sales, profit and margin of the three categories. It also has the lowest loss rate among all three categories. Resourcing, promotional spend, and sales team focus should be weighted toward Technology and high-performing Office Supplies sub-categories, while Furniture volume is allowed to contract in favour of quality over quantity.
04
Seasonal Planning
Inventory and Promotions Should Be Timed Around the Dec–Jan Peak
December and January together account for 19.5% of sales. May to August account for 29.8% across four months, with lower average order-line values ($1,492–$1,632 against $2,018–$2,035 in January and December). Promotional spend and inventory build-up should be front-loaded ahead of December, while the low season offers an opportunity for targeted retention campaigns to smooth revenue volatility.
Methodology

End-to-End Analytical Pipeline

Phase 0
Database Setup
Created the superstore MySQL database with a normalised 4-table schema: orders, customers, returns, regional_managers. Five indexes applied on orders for query performance.
Phase 1
Python ETL Pipeline
Python/SQLAlchemy ETL pipeline reads all sheets from the Excel source, standardises column names, applies schema alignment transformations, and loads 8,399 order lines + 5,496 customers + 572 returns into MySQL.
Phase 2
Exploration & Cleaning
Inspected dtypes, null distribution, and unique values. Engineered year, month, discount bucket, and is_loss columns. Documented 63 null values in product_base_margin as the only data quality gap.
Phase 3
10 Business Questions
Self-generated 10 business questions covering revenue trend, margin analysis, discount impact, shipping efficiency, segment profitability, regional performance, loss rate, seasonality, and returns patterns.
Phase 4
SQL Analysis
Each question answered with production-quality SQL: CASE bucketing, LEFT JOINs for returns, ROUND and pct calculations, and documented RATIONALE + ANSWER comments per query.
Phase 5
Python Visualisation
10 Matplotlib/Seaborn charts — dual-axis bar charts, horizontal bar charts, annotated margin comparisons, and a seasonal line chart with fill. Each chart paired with a plain-language key finding.
